# ADR-011: Spec-judge verdicts are sticky per acceptance-criterion text

- **Decision area:** D7, checks (supersedes the stage-1 judge rule of [ADR-007](ADR-007.md))
- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-21)

## Context

ADR-007 let the Haiku judge fail the stage-1 check: every READY AC must be judged testable, an AC
judged untestable is sent back to the analyst once, and a second failure stops the run.

Run [44890139146](https://github.com/AvAdiii/markdown/actions/runs/44890139146) (issue #4, F2)
stopped at requirements because of that rule, on a specification that was otherwise sound:

| Attempt | ACs | Judged untestable |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 9 | AC-FR-003-3 ("a document", baseline not written down; a fair objection) |
| 1 | 10 | AC-FR-002-2, AC-FR-002-3 |

The analyst rewrote AC-FR-003-3 as asked. AC-FR-002-2 and AC-FR-002-3 were **word for word the same**
in both attempts, and the judge had called both testable in attempt 0
(`checks/spec-0.json`: "the exact expected attribute id=\"custom\" are stated"). Sampling the
judge again on unchanged text gave a different answer, so the retry was judged against a moving
target and the budget of one retry ran out. The flaw is in the rule, not the model: a single
stochastic sample was treated as a deterministic gate and re-drawn on every attempt.

## Decision

1. The judge's verdict is cached per AC **text** for the whole run (`state.json`, `judge_cache`, so
   a resumed attempt keeps it). On a retry only new or reworded ACs are sent to the judge;
   unchanged ACs keep their first verdict. `checks/spec-N.json` records which ACs were judged in
   that attempt (`judged_now`) and marks cached verdicts.
2. Everything else in ADR-007 stays: lint is deterministic and must pass; an untestable verdict on a
   new or changed AC still fails the attempt and is fed back to the analyst; the budget is still
   one retry.

## Alternatives considered

- **Make the judge purely advisory (record, never fail).** Loses the catch it does provide: its
  attempt-0 objection to AC-FR-003-3 was right, and the analyst's fix made that AC testable.
- **Majority vote of three judge samples per AC.** Reduces the variance but triples the judge cost
  on every attempt, and three samples can still flip on a retry.
- **Raise the retry budget.** Treats the symptom; each extra retry re-draws every verdict and can
  fail on a different AC.

## Consequences

- A retry converges: the set of ACs that can fail shrinks to the ones the analyst changed.
- An AC the judge wrongly accepted on attempt 0 stays accepted. The tester (who must write a test
  per AC that fails on base) and the reviewer are the backstop, as for every other check.
- Evidence: the rerun of issue #4 on the new rule is recorded in `results/runs.md`.
